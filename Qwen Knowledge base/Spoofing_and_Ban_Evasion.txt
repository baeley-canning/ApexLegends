KNOWLEDGE BASE: SPOOFING AND BAN EVASION

Purpose:
Explain what spoofing is, why bans - and especially hardware bans - are
evaded, and how defenders respond when an identity can be faked. It
complements BAN GOVERNANCE AND IDENTITY INTEGRITY and CLIENT INTEGRITY,
ANTI-TAMPER, AND ATTESTATION.

This document is reference knowledge. It describes spoofing and evasion
as categories of behaviour, and the defences against them, from a
defensive and engineering perspective. It deliberately contains no
operational steps, tools, or procurement guidance for spoofing
identifiers or for evading a ban.

Evasion techniques and defences change constantly, so treat each
statement as a documented CLAIM. Details are given "as of October 2026".

Related documents: BAN GOVERNANCE AND IDENTITY INTEGRITY, CLIENT
INTEGRITY, ANTI-TAMPER, AND ATTESTATION, CHEAT ECONOMICS AND THE
UNDERGROUND MARKET, HARDWARE, DMA, AND BELOW-KERNEL THREATS, EVADING
ANTI-CHEAT - CATEGORIES AND COUNTERMEASURES, and the numbered
technical series.

---

1. WHAT "SPOOFING" MEANS
To spoof is to present a signal as something it is not.
In anti-cheat the signal is usually an identity: who or what this is.
Spoofing is not one trick; it is a class of techniques across many
layers.
It matters because most enforcement depends on being able to identify
who is responsible.

---

2. IDENTITY IS COMPOSITE
A player is identified by many signals, not one.
There is the account, the device, the network, the payment method, and
the behaviour.
Any single signal can be changed; the whole set is harder to change at
once.
Evasion tries to break the links between these signals.

---

3. WHY PEOPLE EVADE BANS
A ban is only a punishment if it sticks.
Where an account is cheap and the incentive is high, a banned player
simply returns.
This is why the economics in CHEAT ECONOMICS AND THE UNDERGROUND MARKET
drive evasion.
The cheaper an identity is, the weaker a ban becomes.

---

4. WHAT A "HARDWARE BAN" IS
A hardware ban targets the machine rather than the account.
It records identifiers the device presents to software.
The idea is that a new account on the same machine is still the same
machine.
It exists precisely because accounts are cheap to replace.

---

5. THE IDENTIFIERS A DEVICE PRESENTS
Devices expose many identifiers to software: serials, addresses, and
capability strings.
These are read by the operating system and passed to the game or its
protection.
They were designed for identification, not for defence.
Anything a program can read, a program can in principle misreport.

---

6. SPOOFING AS A GENERAL TECHNIQUE
Spoofing appears wherever a signal is trusted.
It can target a device identifier, a network address, a process's name,
or even timing.
The common pattern is to make a false signal look like a true one.
Defences therefore try not to trust any single signal in isolation.

---

7. WHY SPOOFING IS POSSIBLE AT ALL
Identifiers are reported by layers that an attacker may control.
At the lower layers that control is strong; at the higher layers it is
weaker but still present.
The deeper an attacker controls, the more convincingly they can lie.
This is the same "observer below the observed" logic, seen from the
attacker's side.

---

8. THE LAYERS OF SPOOFING
Spoofing is usually described by where in the stack the false signal is
produced.
In software, the values returned to the game are altered.
In firmware, the device itself reports something different.
In hardware, a different device stands in for the original.
Each layer is harder for defenders to inspect than the one above it.

---

9. THE DEFENDER'S FIRST RULE
Do not trust one identifier; treat every signal as a claim.
Corroborate across independent signals and over time.
A single suspicious value proves little; a pattern of them proves more.
This is the defensive counterpart to composite identity.

---

10. HARDWARE-BACKED IDENTITY AND ATTESTATION
The strongest fix is a root of trust the attacker cannot reprogram.
Attestation lets a machine prove its state to the server.
See CLIENT INTEGRITY, ANTI-TAMPER, AND ATTESTATION for the mechanism.
Where attestation is weak, spoofing is easy; where it is strong, it is
costly.

---

11. SERVER-SIDE FINGERPRINTING
Defenders can derive identity from many observations the client does not
fully control.
Consistency of behaviour, capability, and reported values forms a
fingerprint.
A fingerprint built server-side is harder to see and harder to fake than
a single local identifier.
It also raises privacy questions, treated below.

---

12. LINKAGE AND GRAPH ANALYSIS
Evasion creates many identities; detection links them.
Relationships between accounts, machines, payments, and timing form a
graph.
Suspicious clusters reveal a returning player even when each signal is
fresh.
Linkage shifts the fight from single identifiers to relationships.

---

13. GRADED AND DELAYED ENFORCEMENT
If evasion is cheap, enforcement should raise its cost.
Detections can be batched into waves so offenders cannot tell what
triggered them.
Some responses mitigate rather than ban: isolated matchmaking or reduced
trust.
Making the safe response cheaper than evasion is the goal.

---

14. THE PRIVACY AND FALSE-POSITIVE TRADE-OFF
Stronger identification collects more about the player.
That raises privacy duties and the risk of punishing the innocent.
A shared or second-hand machine can look like a returning cheater's.
Identity work must therefore be proportionate and correctable.
See TELEMETRY, PRIVACY, AND PLAYER DATA ETHICS.

---

15. SUMMARY
Spoofing is presenting a false signal, usually an identity, and it spans
software, firmware, and hardware layers.
Hardware bans exist because accounts are cheap; they work only if a
device cannot easily become a new device.
Defenders answer with composite identity, attestation, server-side
fingerprinting, and linkage.
Graded, delayed enforcement raises the cost of evasion without harming the
innocent more than necessary.
Because weak identification is easy and strong identification is costly,
this is an arms race measured in economics and trade-offs.

CORE PRINCIPLE:
A ban is only as strong as the identity it attaches to, and an identity
is only as strong as the attacker's cost to change it. Hardware bans
exist because accounts are cheap; spoofing exists because identifiers are
read by software, and anything read can be misreported. The defender's
answer is never one perfect identifier but many independent, corroborated
signals, plus attestation where hardware can be trusted. You do not win
this by being unfakeable; you win by making evasion and re-entry cost more
than playing straight.
