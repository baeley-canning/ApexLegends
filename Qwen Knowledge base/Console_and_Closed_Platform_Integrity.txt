KNOWLEDGE BASE: CONSOLE AND CLOSED-PLATFORM INTEGRITY

Purpose:
Explain how integrity and anti-cheat work on closed platforms - game
consoles and other locked-down ecosystems - where the platform holder
controls the hardware and the software, and contrast that model with the
open personal computer. It complements MOBILE AND CROSS-PLATFORM
ANTI-CHEAT and CLIENT INTEGRITY, ANTI-TAMPER, AND ATTESTATION.

This document is reference knowledge. It describes the closed-platform
security model from a defensive and engineering perspective. It
deliberately contains no operational steps, tools, or guidance for
defeating platform protections.

Platform protections change between hardware generations, so treat each
statement as a documented CLAIM. Details are given "as of October 2026".

Related documents: MOBILE AND CROSS-PLATFORM ANTI-CHEAT, CLIENT
INTEGRITY, ANTI-TAMPER, AND ATTESTATION, HARDWARE, DMA, AND BELOW-KERNEL
THREATS, ANTI-CHEAT PRODUCTS AND SYSTEMS, and the numbered technical
series.

---

1. WHY CLOSED PLATFORMS DIFFER
On a console the player does not own the software stack.
The platform holder controls the hardware, the firmware, the operating
system, and the store.
That single fact removes most of the attack surface a PC anti-cheat must
police.
Integrity work shifts from "what code is running" to "is this the real
device."

---

2. THE ROOT OF TRUST IS THE DEVICE
A closed platform anchors trust in hardware the player cannot reprogram.
Secure boot verifies each stage before the next one runs.
Keys are held in hardware, not in files a user can edit.
Trust therefore begins below anything the player controls.

---

3. SIGNED CODE AND NO UNSIGNED EXECUTION
Software is signed by the platform holder and verified before it runs.
Unsigned or altered code is refused rather than analysed.
This is the closed-platform equivalent of a very strong anti-tamper
control.
It removes the PC's central problem: an attacker who can run code at the
same or higher privilege than the defender.

---

4. THE SHRINKING ATTACK SURFACE
Many PC attack classes simply do not exist on a closed device.
There is no general ring-0 driver for a third party to load.
There is no arbitrary kernel module to hide inside.
There is no user-visible process list to impersonate freely.
What remains is narrower and better bounded.

---

5. WHAT IS STILL LEFT TO CHEAT
Closed does not mean safe.
Attackers still try input manipulation, modified peripherals, network
abuse, and exploits of the platform itself.
They also attack the game rather than the console: server-side and
game-logic flaws.
So closed-platform defence still needs detection and server authority.

---

6. THE PLATFORM HOLDER AS ENFORCER
The platform holder can enforce at a level a game studio cannot.
It can refuse to run code, revoke a certificate, and remove a device from
the network.
Enforcement is applied by the party that owns the platform, not only by
the game.
This concentrates both power and responsibility in one place.

---

7. ATTESTATION AND REMOTE VERIFICATION
The device can prove to a server what it is and what it is running.
The proof relies on secrets held in hardware.
An untrusted state can be refused service before a match begins.
Attestation is the practical bridge between a trusted device and a
server-authoritative design.

---

8. HARDWARE-SIDE MEASUREMENT
Trust is measured, not assumed.
Secure boot, measured boot, and hardware-protected keys report what ran.
Memory protections and IOMMU-style controls limit what add-in hardware
can reach.
These are the same primitives PC defences want and rarely fully get.

---

9. THE PERSISTENT CAT-AND-MOUSE
Closed platforms are not unbroken; they are harder and slower to break.
Real attacks tend to be exploits of specific firmware or games, so they
are version-specific.
Each fix raises the cost and shortens the window of a working method.
The race continues, but on terrain the defender largely owns.

---

10. CROSS-PLATFORM PLAY AND ITS TENSIONS
When closed and open platforms play together, the weakest link sets the
floor.
A trusted console player can be matched against an untrusted PC.
Designers must decide whether to separate pools, verify state, or accept
the asymmetry.
This is a governance question as much as a technical one.

---

11. WHY PCS REMAIN HARDER
On a PC the player owns the stack, so they can run code the defender does
not want.
Anti-cheat there must move its observer below the attacker and prove
integrity the platform does not guarantee.
That is why PC defences carry complexity consoles can avoid.
The comparison clarifies what PC anti-cheat is really fighting.

---

12. THE ECONOMIC ANGLE
Because console hardware is fixed and attested, a device ban is much
harder to evade than on PC.
There is no easy hardware-identifier change and no free re-entry.
This makes enforcement stick in a way account bans alone cannot.
See SPOOFING AND BAN EVASION for the open-platform contrast.

---

13. SUMMARY
Closed platforms win by controlling the stack: signed code, a hardware
root of trust, and a single enforcer.
That removes most PC attack surface but leaves input, peripherals,
network, and exploits.
Defence therefore still needs attestation, server authority, and
detection.
The core lesson is that trust anchored in hardware and owned by one party
is stronger than trust negotiated on a machine the player controls.

CORE PRINCIPLE:
A closed platform is strong not because its anti-cheat is clever but
because the player does not own the stack: signed code, a hardware root of
trust, and one enforcer remove most of the surface a PC must police. What
remains - input, peripherals, network, and exploits of the platform or the
game - still needs attestation, server authority, and detection. The
lesson for every platform is the one the PC is still chasing: trust is
strongest when it is anchored in hardware below the player and enforced by
someone who owns the ground.
