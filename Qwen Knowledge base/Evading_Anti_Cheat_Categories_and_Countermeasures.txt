KNOWLEDGE BASE: EVADING ANTI-CHEAT - CATEGORIES AND COUNTERMEASURES

Purpose:
Survey how players and cheat authors try to avoid, defeat, or delay
anti-cheat detection and enforcement, organised by the layer of the
defence each family targets, and pair each with the response defenders
use. It complements ADAPTIVE CHEATING, RED TEAMING, AND EVOLVING
ANTI-CHEAT SYSTEMS and HOW ANTI-CHEATS WORK - VANGUARD, BATTLTEYE, EAC,
AND JAVELIN.

This document is reference knowledge. It describes evasion as categories
of behaviour and the countermeasures against them, from a defensive and
engineering perspective. It deliberately contains no operational steps,
tools, or product-specific bypass detail.

Evasion and detection both change constantly, so treat each statement as
a documented CLAIM. Details are given "as of October 2026".

Related documents: ADAPTIVE CHEATING, RED TEAMING, AND EVOLVING
ANTI-CHEAT SYSTEMS, ANTI-CHEAT ARCHITECTURE, CLIENT INTEGRITY,
ANTI-TAMPER, AND ATTESTATION, SPOOFING AND BAN EVASION, HOW ANTI-CHEATS
WORK - VANGUARD, BATTLTEYE, EAC, AND JAVELIN, and the numbered technical
series.

---

1. WHAT "GETTING AROUND" MEANS
Getting around anti-cheat means achieving one of three things: not being
detected, not being enforced, or not being punished for long.
These are different problems with different techniques.
A cheat that is undetectable and one that is detectable but unpunished
are both "getting around" the intent of the system.
This document treats them as one family of behaviours.

---

2. THE UNIVERSAL PATTERN
Almost every evasion reduces to one of four moves.
HIDE: do not let the observer see the act.
CONFUSE: let it see, but not believe.
OUTRUN: act only when the observer is off duty.
IMPERSONATE: look like something trusted.
Naming the move is more useful than naming the technique.

---

3. EVADING SIGNATURE DETECTION
Signature detection matches known bad artifacts.
It is evaded by changing the artifact so the known pattern no longer
fits.
This is cheap once a signature is public, so signatures age quickly.
The defender's answer is to rely less on any single artifact and to
combine signatures with behaviour and integrity.

---

4. EVADING BEHAVIOUR ANALYSIS
Behaviour detection looks at what a player does, not only what they run.
It is evaded by making the cheating behave more like legitimate play:
occasional mistakes, human-like timing, varied results.
This is the "humanising" problem, and it is fundamentally about
statistical plausibility.
The defender's answer is richer context and cross-player, cross-session
patterns, not a single threshold.

---

5. HIDING FROM THE OBSERVER
An attacker can try to act where the observer cannot see: at a lower
privilege level, a different process, or outside the monitor's scope.
This is the same "observer below the observed" race, from the attacker's
side.
The defence is to move the observer lower and widen its scope: kernel,
hypervisor, and hardware roots.
What cannot be hidden from a lower observer must instead be made costly.

---

6. TAMPERING WITH THE OBSERVER
Rather than hide from the monitor, an attacker may attack it directly:
disable, alter, or confuse it.
That turns a detection problem into a self-protection problem.
Defences include loading early, protecting the monitor's own integrity,
keeping critical checks off the client, and validating from the server.
An observer that can be switched off is not an observer.

---

7. EVADING INTEGRITY AND ATTESTATION
Integrity and attestation try to prove the client is unchanged and the
platform is genuine.
They are evaded by presenting a false but consistent picture, or by
attesting through a layer the attacker controls.
See CLIENT INTEGRITY, ANTI-TAMPER, AND ATTESTATION for the mechanisms.
The defence is a trust anchor the attacker cannot reprogram, plus
corroboration from the server.

---

8. BLENDING IN AND "HUMANISING" INPUT
Automation is often obvious because it is too precise or too consistent.
Evasion makes automated input look human: jitter, delay, variability, and
errors.
This targets detection that looks for superhuman precision or regularity.
The defence is to look at distributions and consistency over time rather
than at single events.

---

9. TIMING AND SCHEDULING EVASION
An attacker can choose when to cheat as well as how.
Cheating sparingly, only in key moments, or only outside review windows
reduces exposure.
It also complicates ban waves, because the behaviour is intermittent.
The defence is to reason over long windows and to keep some detection
hidden, so timing cannot be optimised against it.

---

10. IDENTITY ROTATION AND RE-ENTRY
Detection is only as useful as enforcement, and enforcement needs an
identity to attach to.
Rotating accounts, devices, and networks turns one offender into many
apparently new players.
See SPOOFING AND BAN EVASION for the identity mechanics.
The defence is linkage across signals and enforcement that raises the
cost of re-entry.

---

11. MOVING THE CHEAT OFF THE MACHINE
If the machine is watched closely, an attacker can put the advantage
elsewhere.
Reading the game from another device, or driving input through external
hardware, keeps the main machine clean.
This targets client-side monitoring that only sees its own host.
The defence reaches the boundaries: bus and device-level checks,
attestation of the whole platform, and server-side plausibility.

---

12. EVADING NETWORK-LEVEL CHECKS
Some advantage comes from abusing timing and shared state rather than
from memory.
Timing abuse is hard to separate from a poor connection, which is itself
a form of camouflage.
See NETWORK AND LAG MANIPULATION for the mechanisms.
The defence is server authority over timing and state, not client trust.

---

13. THE DEFENDER'S PLAYBOOK
No single answer covers all of the above, so defences are layered.
Use several independent signals, so confusing one is not enough.
Keep critical decisions on the server, so hiding on the client is not
enough.
Anchor trust in hardware and attestation, so impersonation is costly.
Link identities, so rotation is not enough.
Keep some detection hidden and act in waves, so timing is not enough.
Each layer removes one evasion's payoff.

---

14. WHY THERE IS NO PERMANENT WIN
Every defence is a cost that an attacker can pay, given enough incentive.
Every evasion is a signal a defender can learn to read.
The loop is the point: adaptation is continuous, not a finish line.
The realistic goal is to keep evasion more expensive than honest play.

---

15. SUMMARY
Evasion aims to go unseen, unenforced, or unpunished, which are different
problems.
Its moves are few - hide, confuse, outrun, impersonate - and reusable
across layers.
Each family targets a specific defence: signatures, behaviour, integrity,
input, timing, identity, host, or network.
The defender's answer always has the same shape: layer independent
signals, decide on the server, anchor in hardware, link identities, and
act in waves.
Because both sides adapt, the aim is to keep evasion costlier than
playing straight, not to end the race.

CORE PRINCIPLE:
Getting around anti-cheat is not one trick but four moves - hide, confuse,
outrun, impersonate - aimed at whichever layer is weakest. So a defence
survives not by being unbreakable but by having no single weak layer to
aim at: many independent signals, decisions on the server, trust anchored
in hardware, identities linked, and enforcement that stays hidden and
delayed. You do not stop evasion; you make it cost more than playing
honestly, and you keep making it cost more as the other side adapts.
