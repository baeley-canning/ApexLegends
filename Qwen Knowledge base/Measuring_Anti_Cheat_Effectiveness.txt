KNOWLEDGE BASE: MEASURING ANTI-CHEAT EFFECTIVENESS

Purpose:
Explain how to judge whether an anti-cheat actually works: what
"effective" means, why detection rate alone is misleading, and how to
design honest measurement. It complements the numbered STATISTICS AND
EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN and ADVERSARIAL AI AND CHEAT DETECTION.

This document is reference knowledge. It describes measurement and
evaluation from a defensive, engineering perspective, using publicly
documented practice. It contains no instructions for defeating any
system.

Claims and designs change, so treat each statement as a documented
CLAIM. Details are given "as of October 2026".

Related documents: STATISTICS AND EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN, ADVERSARIAL AI AND
CHEAT DETECTION, ANTI-CHEAT PRODUCTS AND SYSTEMS, BAN GOVERNANCE AND
IDENTITY INTEGRITY, and the numbered technical series.

---

1. WHY MEASUREMENT IS HARD
The thing being measured is adversarial and partly hidden.
You cannot count cheats you never detect.
Success is often invisible: nothing happens.
This makes anti-cheat unusually hard to evaluate honestly.

---

2. WHAT "EFFECTIVE" MEANS
Effectiveness is not one number.
It includes detection, timeliness, fairness, and cost.
A system can be strong on one axis and weak on another.
Agreeing on the axes is the first step to measuring any of them.

---

3. DETECTION RATE AND ITS LIMITS
Detection rate is how many known cheats are caught.
Its denominator is always incomplete, because unknown cheats are
excluded.
A high rate against known cheats says little about unknown ones.
It is a useful but narrow signal.

---

4. FALSE POSITIVES AND FALSE NEGATIVES
False positives punish honest players; false negatives miss cheats.
Both have costs, and they trade against each other.
Tuning to reduce one usually raises the other.
Reporting only one of them is a misleading summary.

---

5. THE COST ASYMMETRY
A false positive can destroy a player's trust and career; a false
negative lets a cheat last longer.
The costs are not symmetric, so the acceptable balance is not a coin
flip.
Most systems rightly prefer to tolerate some misses over some false bans.
Stating that preference is part of honest measurement.

---

6. LATENCY OF DETECTION
How fast a cheat is caught matters as much as whether it is caught.
A detection after months of damage is worth less than one in days.
Ban-wave timing is a deliberate trade between speed and coverage.
Detection latency is therefore a first-class metric.

---

7. ACCURACY CLAIMS AND WHAT THEY OMIT
Vendors sometimes cite very high accuracy figures.
Such figures usually describe a specific dataset, period, or task.
They rarely state the false-positive rate in production or the unknown
cheats missed.
A claim is only as meaningful as its denominator and its scope.

---

8. GROUND TRUTH AND LABELS
Measurement needs labels, and labels need certainty.
Confirmed cheats come from bans, confessions, or forensic review.
Unknown behaviour is unlabelled and biases every metric.
Weak ground truth is the deepest limit of anti-cheat evaluation.

---

9. EVALUATION DESIGN
Good evaluation states the population, the period, and the definition of
a cheat.
It separates training data from test data to avoid self-congratulation.
It reports false positives and false negatives together.
See the numbered STATISTICS AND EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN for the underlying
principles.

---

10. CONTROLLED ROLLOUTS
New detections are often tested on a controlled subset before wide use.
This measures real-world impact while limiting harm from mistakes.
Rollouts can be paused if false positives rise.
Controlled deployment is measurement and safety at once.

---

11. DRIFT AND DECAY
Cheats and players change, so a detector's accuracy decays over time.
A model tuned to last year's cheats may be weak against this year's.
Continuous monitoring and retraining are part of effectiveness, not
extras.
See ADVERSARIAL AI AND CHEAT DETECTION for drift in detail.

---

12. METRICS THAT MATTER
Useful metrics include confirmed detections, confirmed false positives,
time-to-detection, appeal-overturn rate, and coverage across cheat
classes.
Together they describe a system better than any single accuracy figure.
Metrics should be published with their definitions and their limits.
Honest metrics build more trust than impressive ones.

---

13. SUMMARY
Anti-cheat is hard to measure because the target is adversarial and
partly invisible.
Effectiveness spans detection, timeliness, fairness, and cost, not one
number.
Detection rate omits unknown cheats, and false positives trade against
false negatives.
Accuracy claims are meaningful only with their denominator, scope, and
false-positive rate.
Weak ground truth and decay set the real limits of any evaluation.

CORE PRINCIPLE:
You cannot prove an anti-cheat works by counting the cheats it caught,
because the ones that matter are the ones it never saw. Judge it on
several axes at once - detections, false positives, time-to-detection,
appeals, and coverage - and always ask what the number's denominator is.
Honest measurement, including its own blind spots, is worth more than an
impressive figure.
