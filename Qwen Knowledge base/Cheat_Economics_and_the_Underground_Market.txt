KNOWLEDGE BASE: CHEAT ECONOMICS AND THE UNDERGROUND MARKET

Purpose:
Explain the business behind cheating software - how cheats are sold,
priced, distributed, and sustained - and why the market keeps existing
despite enforcement. It complements CHEATING IN GAMES - HISTORY,
TAXONOMY, AND DETECTION and BAN GOVERNANCE AND IDENTITY INTEGRITY.

This document is reference knowledge. It describes the market and its
incentives from an analytical, defensive perspective. It contains no
instructions for buying, building, or operating any cheat.

The market changes constantly and much of it is deliberately hidden, so
treat each statement as a documented CLAIM. Details are given "as of
October 2026".

Related documents: BAN GOVERNANCE AND IDENTITY INTEGRITY, CHEATING IN
GAMES - HISTORY, TAXONOMY, AND DETECTION, ADAPTIVE CHEATING, RED TEAM,
AND EVOLVING SYSTEMS, SPOOFING AND BAN EVASION, and the numbered
technical series.

---

1. WHY ECONOMICS MATTERS
Cheating persists because it is profitable, not because it is easy.
Understanding the incentives explains why some defences fail and what
actually raises cost.
A purely technical view of anti-cheat misses half the problem.
Economics is the other half.

---

2. THE SHAPE OF THE MARKET
The market is a layered supply chain: developers, distributors,
resellers, and support.
Each layer adds margin and distance from the user.
That distance protects the people at the top from consequence.
Understanding the layers is the first step to understanding the
incentives.

---

3. CHEAT-AS-A-SERVICE
Most sellers do not ship a one-time product; they run a subscription
service.
The service must keep working after every anti-cheat update, or customers
leave.
This turns cheating into an ongoing operations business, not a one-off
sale.
It also aligns the seller's interest with staying undetected, which
drives continuous adaptation.

---

4. PRICING AND TIERS
Sellers often offer tiers: basic, premium, private, and invite-only.
Higher tiers trade exclusivity and lower detection risk for a higher
price.
Low-tier cheats are cheap and widely detected; high-tier ones are
expensive and quieter.
The tiering itself is a detection signal: widespread, cheap cheats are
the easiest to catch.

---

5. THE DETECTION-ADAPTATION CYCLE AS A PRODUCT
Every ban wave is a service event for the seller: it prompts updates and
sometimes new sales.
Detections are absorbed and worked around, then marketed as improved
stealth.
This is the arms race seen through a commercial lens.
See ADAPTIVE CHEATING, RED TEAM, AND EVOLVING SYSTEMS for the technical
side.

---

6. HWID SPOOFERS AND BAN-EVASION SERVICES
Because bans often target the machine, sellers offer spoofers that change
device identifiers.
These are sold alongside cheats as an insurance product.
They attack the enforcement layer rather than the detection layer.
They are also why identity friction, not just detection, matters.

---

7. RESELLERS AND DISTRIBUTION
Cheats reach users through resellers, forums, and private channels.
Reselling decentralises the business and dilutes accountability.
It also spreads malware, because buyers cannot verify what they receive.
The trust problem inside the market mirrors the trust problem the market
exploits.

---

8. GOLD, BOOSTING, AND REAL-MONEY TRADING
Not all market value is in code; ranks, items, and accounts are traded
too.
This overlaps with matchmaking integrity and the account economy.
It links cheating to fraud and, in some cases, to organised crime.
The money flow, not the code, is often the durable part.

---

9. THE DEVELOPMENT SIDE
Cheat developers range from hobbyists to organised teams with release
schedules.
Sustained operation requires ongoing research into each anti-cheat.
That requirement is why high-end cheats cost as much as they do.
It is also the market's main weakness: skilled, sustained effort is
scarce.

---

10. LAW ENFORCEMENT AND LITIGATION
Publishers have sued cheat sellers and referred some cases to
authorities.
Litigation raises the cost and risk of operating in the open.
It works best against large, visible sellers rather than diffuse ones.
Its main value is deterrence and disruption, not elimination.

---

11. PLATFORM AND PAYMENT PRESSURE
Removing a seller's payment processing or hosting is often more effective
than a technical countermeasure.
Platforms, storefronts, and payment providers add friction the seller
cannot engineer around.
This is economics used as defence: attack the business, not just the
binary.
It complements, but does not replace, technical detection.

---

12. WHY THE MARKET PERSISTS
As long as some players value winning more than the rules, demand
exists.
Low barriers to entry and high margins sustain supply.
Enforcement raises cost but does not remove the incentive.
This is why the market is a permanent feature, not a passing nuisance.

---

13. WHAT ACTUALLY RAISES THE COST
The effective levers are economic: account friction, identity
requirements, payment and hosting pressure, and litigation against large
sellers.
Technical detection raises the seller's operating cost, which is the
point.
Combining technical and economic pressure is stronger than either alone.
Neither alone ends the market; together they bend its economics.

---

14. SUMMARY
Cheating persists because it is profitable, so anti-cheat is partly an
economic problem.
The market is layered, subscription-based, tiered, and supported by
ban-evasion services.
Detection drives adaptation, which sellers sell as improved stealth.
The strongest levers beyond detection are identity friction, payment and
hosting pressure, and litigation against large sellers.
The market is permanent while demand exists; the goal is to raise its
cost, not to imagine its disappearance.

CORE PRINCIPLE:
Cheating is a business, so it obeys business logic: it survives by
staying profitable, not by staying undetected. Technical detection raises
the seller's costs, but the durable levers are economic - making accounts
dear, cutting off payments and hosting, and suing the largest sellers.
Fight the market's economics and its code at the same time; either alone
only moves the cost around.
